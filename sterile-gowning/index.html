Sterile Gown and Gloves

Two tracks: gowning and gloving yourself with the closed-glove technique, and gowning and gloving the next person once you're sterile. In a bedside opening, two or three people may need to do this fast.

The scrub RN or tech does this first. Closed gloving keeps your bare hands inside the sleeves the whole time. STS 2017 · AORN

Once you're gowned and gloved, you can gown and glove the surgeon or the next team member. Your gloves never touch their skin. AORN

  1. PPE on, then hand hygiene

    • Cap, mask and face shield go on first. They aren't sterile, so they come before your hands are clean.
    • Hand hygiene, then the gown. SCU 10-214

  2. Open onto a sterile surface

    • The circulator opens the gown pack and the glove wrapper onto a clean, dry surface without reaching over them.
    • Check the gown and glove sizes before the packs are opened.

  3. Pick it up by the inside of the neckline

    • Lift the folded gown straight up and step back into open space so it unfolds without touching anything.
    • Inside faces you. Touch only the inside. If the gown brushes anything non-sterile, get a new one.

  4. Arms in, hands stay inside the cuffs

    • Slide both arms into the sleeves and stop with your fingers just inside the cuff edge.
    • Keeping your hands covered is what makes closed gloving work.

  5. The circulator ties the back

    • The circulator reaches inside from behind, pulls the gown up onto your shoulders, and ties the neck and the inner waist ties.
    • They touch only the inside and back of the gown.

  6. First glove: palm-down, fingers to the elbow

    • Through the sleeve, pick up the first glove and lay it on the sleeve-covered palm of the other hand.
    • Glove palm-down, fingers pointing toward your elbow, thumb over thumb. Grip the bottom edge of the cuff through the sleeve.

  7. Cuff over, fingers in

    • With the other sleeved hand, stretch the top of the glove cuff over the back of your hand and the gown cuff.
    • Pull the sleeve and glove up together as your fingers work into the glove.

  8. Second glove, then double

    • Repeat on the other hand, using the gloved hand to help.
    • Put a second pair on over the first: AORN recommends double gloving when blood exposure is likely. AORN

  9. Turn the gown with the card

    • Pull the card off the front tie and hand the card to a team member.
    • Turn in place, take back only the tie (never the card) and tie it at your side.

  10. Know your sterile zones

    • Sterile: the front from chest to the level of the field, and the sleeves from 2 inches above the elbow to the cuff. AORN
    • Not sterile: the back, the neckline, the underarms and anything below the field. Keep your hands in front, above the waist and below the shoulders; don't tuck them under your arms.

  1. Hold the next gown open

    • You're already gowned and gloved. Pick up the next gown by the neckline and let it unfold with the inside facing the person.
    • Cuff your gloved hands under the gown's shoulder folds so your gloves stay covered.

  2. Arms in, hands stay in the cuffs

    • They step in and slide their arms into the sleeves, keeping their hands inside the cuffs.
    • The circulator ties the back.

  3. Stretch the glove open

    • Hold the right glove with the palm facing them and the thumb toward their thumb. Your fingers go under the cuff, thumbs out, so your gloves stay covered.
    • They push the sleeved hand down into the glove. Let the cuff snap over the gown cuff. This is closed assisted gloving; AORN reserves open assisted gloving for when closed isn't possible. AORN

  4. Second glove, then turn

    • Repeat for the left hand; their gloved right hand can help pull the cuff.
    • They turn the gown with the card, the same way as in the first track.
Contaminated? If a gown or glove touches anything non-sterile, say so out loud and replace it. A circulator can pull a contaminated glove off; re-glove with assistance from a sterile team member. Then continue.
