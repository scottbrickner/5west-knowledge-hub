U-Drape for Bedside Sternotomy

How to open and place the Medline orthopedic split drape so the U frames the sternum from notch to xiphoid. Follow the arrows printed on the drape; they do most of the thinking for you.

Before the drape touches the patient

  • Gown/dressing off; expose neck to umbilicus and side to side.
  • Move ECG electrodes off the sternum; keep defib pads and pacing wires connected.
  • Skin prep with povidone-iodine only, scrubbed over the incision and wide across the chest (SCU 10-214).
  • No CHG/alcohol prep sticks: their alcohol vapor is fuel under the drape. Blot any pooling at the sides or under the shoulders.
  • Pack opened onto the sterile field or handed to the sterile team member.
  • Coordinate with the compressor and surgeon; prep and drape around CPR unless told to pause.

  1. Check the pack

    • Confirm REF DYNJP8301, the orthopedic split drape with the 6" × 40" split.
    • Package intact, dry and in date. Torn or wet means not sterile; grab another.
    • Open with sterile technique onto the field or to the gloved, gowned team member.
    Look for ⊔ split diagram on the label

  2. Find the split end

    • The folded drape is printed SPLIT END with an arrow.
    • That arrow points to the patient's head. Orient it before you lean over the chest.
    • Handle it by the folded edges only.
    Look for ⇧ Split end

  3. Line the edge up with the incision

    • Lay the folded drape on the prepped chest with its left edge on the sternal incision line and the top edge at the sternal notch.
    • Arrow still pointing to the head.
    • Get the edge right now. Sliding the drape later drags non-sterile skin into the field.
    The pack is folded off-center on purpose. With that edge on the incision, the first side-to-side unfold leaves the drape centered over the sternum.

  4. Unfold side to side

    • First unfold: open the pack across the incision. Because the edge started on the incision, it lands centered as three panels.
    • The side panels are labeled UNFOLD with arrows pointing out.
    • Open each side panel toward the patient's arm.
    • Cuff your gloved hands inside the fold as you open it.
    Look for ⇦ Unfold Unfold ⇨

  5. Unfold toward the feet

    • With the drape now spanning arm to arm, follow the UNFOLD ⇩ arrow.
    • Open it down over the abdomen and legs, covering the chest tubes and drainage.
    • Anything that drops below the bed edge is no longer sterile. Let it go; don't pull it back up.
    Look for Unfold ⇩

  6. Find the U and its tape

    • The split section is still folded, with its adhesive covered by white printed liners along both tails and across the base of the U.
    • The white tabs lower on the drape are cord holders for suction and cautery lines.
    • Don't peel anything until the U is sitting where you want it.
    Look for Medline liners

  7. Peel and open the split

    • Peel the liners off the base and both tails.
    • Open the split tails up toward the head, one on each side of the sternum and neck.
    • Press the adhesive edges to dry skin, working from the base of the U upward.
    Adhesive only grips dry skin. If prep is still wet, the edge lifts and the field leaks.

  8. Check the window

    • The U frames the sternum from notch to xiphoid, with skin visible on both sides of midline.
    • Tails lie flat up beside the neck; ETT and lines stay outside the field at the head.
    • Edges sealed, no gaps, nothing tented over the incision line.
    • Call out "draped" so the team knows the chest is ready.

Where it goes wrong

Upside downThe U ends up over the abdomen. Check the SPLIT END arrow before the drape touches skin.
Off centerOne edge of the window sits on the sternum. In step 3, set the pack's left edge on the incision line, not the middle of the pack; don't slide it after.
Peeled too earlyTape grabs the wrong spot and can't be moved cleanly. Peel only once the U is lined up.
Reaching acrossHands and sleeves stay above the field and in front of you. Walk around instead.

Fire risk

The package carries a flammability caution, and this procedure brings cautery, defibrillation and 100% oxygen together under a drape. That is why Keck preps with povidone-iodine only. A CHG stick leaves alcohol vapor trapped under the drape. Keep the prep off the linens and blot any pooling before the drape goes on.
